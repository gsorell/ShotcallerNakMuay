Shot Caller vs. FightFlow vs. Heavy Bag Pro: Which Muay Thai App is Best?

By Shotcaller Sam
August 25, 2026

If you are looking to elevate your solo heavy bag or shadow boxing sessions, the top digital tools on the market consistently boil down to three options: FightFlow, Heavy Bag Pro, and Shot Caller Nak Muay.

Each app takes a completely different philosophical approach to combat sports training. Which one should you install? Here is an honest, feature-by-feature breakdown.

The High-Level Differences

  • FightFlow: A highly polished, gamified app designed heavily around western boxing and cardio-kickboxing volume.
  • Heavy Bag Pro: A library of static, pre-recorded audio workouts styled like a digital fitness class.
  • Shot Caller Nak Muay: A dynamic, infinitely generating audio callout engine built specifically around authentic Muay Thai styles and terminology.

Feature Comparison Breakdown

Feature Shot Caller Nak Muay FightFlow Heavy Bag Pro
Combo Generation Infinite, dynamic algorithm Dynamic, boxing-heavy Static, pre-recorded tracks
Authentic Thai Terms Yes (Teep, Sok, Khao, Check) No (Numbers & Generic Kicks) No (Standard Kickboxing)

1. Callout Authenticity & Terminology

If you train in a real Muay Thai gym, you are used to a coach yelling "Teep," "Check," or "Elbow." FightFlow and Heavy Bag Pro primarily lean on standard western boxing translation: calling out a "1-2-3-Right Kick."

Shot Caller was built for Nak Muays. The audio engine uses the actual terminology of the sport, and equally importantly, calls for defensive maneuvers like checks and slips mid-combo, forcing you to maintain a defensive mindset.

2. Reactive Training vs. Rote Memorization

Heavy Bag Pro excels if you don't want to think; you just press play on a 30-minute track and follow the coach. The downside is that you are listening to a static MP3 file. Once you've done a track a few times, you anticipate the calls.

Both FightFlow and Shot Caller use procedural generation, meaning no two rounds are ever the same. However, Shot Caller takes this a step further by grouping combos into actual Thai fighting styles (such as the aggressive, low-kicking Muay Mat or the technical Muay Femur).

Final Thought

There is no "wrong" app here—it depends on your goals. For a sweat-inducing cardio workout, FightFlow is fantastic. For guided classes, Heavy Bag Pro is unmatched. But if you want a tool that authentically replicates an experienced Thai pad-holder challenging your timing, defense, and technique, Shot Caller Nak Muay is the clear choice.
